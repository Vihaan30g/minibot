Minibot: Indoor Autonomous Navigation Robot Full documentation coming soon

A differential-drive robot that I designed and built from scratch, mechanics and electronics included, to navigate autonomously indoors.

View repository on GitHub

About the robot

Minibot is an indoor autonomous navigation robot with two differentially driven wheels and two caster wheels. It senses the world with a ZED 2i stereo camera, computes on an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin, and reads two wheel encoders through a single ESP32 microcontroller. The ZED 2i sits on a mount whose height can be adjusted.

The chassis is made of wood (plywood sheets and wooden rods) together with 3D-printed parts. The whole robot, both the mechanical design and the electronics, was designed and built by me.

DriveDifferential drive: 2 driven wheels and 2 caster wheels
ComputerNVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin
CameraZED 2i, adjustable height
Odometry sensors2 wheel encoders
MicrocontrollerESP32
ChassisPlywood sheets, wooden rods and 3D-printed parts

How it was built

  1. Simulation first. Before building anything physical, I created a complete, working simulation of the robot in Gazebo Ignition. In simulation it reaches good autonomy using Nav2 for navigation and RTAB-Map for SLAM.
  2. Then the real robot. I built the physical Minibot myself. It is complete and drives well, with its motor controller working. Running SLAM and the navigation stack on the real robot is the next step.
